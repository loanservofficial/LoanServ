## Why a Loan Against Property?

A **loan against property (LAP)** lets you borrow against a house, flat or commercial property you already own, while you keep using it. Because the loan is secured, it usually carries a lower rate and a longer tenure than an unsecured personal or business loan. That makes it a common choice for business owners in Vijayawada and Visakhapatnam who need working capital, want to expand, or want to consolidate expensive debt.

If you are deciding between the two, see [loan against property vs personal loan](/blog/loan-against-property-vs-personal-loan).

## How Much Can You Borrow?

Lenders look at two limits and give you the **lower** of the two:

1. **Loan-to-value (LTV).** A percentage of the property's market value as assessed by the lender's valuer. For LAP it is typically well below 100%, often somewhere around half to two-thirds of the value. Residential property usually gets a higher LTV than commercial property.
2. **Your repayment capacity.** Your income, existing EMIs and the proposed tenure determine the EMI a lender will allow.

So a valuable property with modest income can still limit the loan amount. Try the [LAP calculator](/calculators/lap) to see how value, LTV and tenure interact.

## Property Papers Lenders Ask For in Andhra Pradesh

The property is the lender's security, so the legal check is thorough. Expect to be asked for:

- **Registered sale deed** and the **link documents** showing the chain of ownership over several years.
- **Encumbrance certificate (EC)** for the period the lender specifies, showing no other loans or claims on the property.
- **Approved building plan and layout approval.** In the Vijayawada–Guntur region this may involve the municipal corporation or APCRDA; in Visakhapatnam, GVMC or VMRDA, depending on location.
- **Latest property tax receipts** and, where relevant, electricity bills in the owner's name.
- **Occupancy or completion certificate** for newer buildings, where applicable.
- For flats: the **builder or association NOC** if requested.

Properties in unapproved layouts, those with plan deviations, properties with unclear inheritance (for example, a missing legal-heir certificate after an owner's death), and agricultural land are the most common reasons a LAP application is delayed or declined. Sorting these out **before** you apply saves weeks.

## Documents About You

- KYC for all applicants and co-owners (every owner of the property usually has to be a co-applicant).
- **Self-employed:** ITRs for the last two to three years, profit and loss statement and balance sheet, GST returns if applicable, and business bank statements.
- **Salaried:** recent salary slips, Form 16 and salary account statements.
- Details of existing loans and a clean repayment history. Check your [credit score](/blog/what-is-a-good-credit-score-in-india) first.

## What Affects Your Rate and Approval

- **Type of property.** Self-occupied residential property is generally viewed most favourably.
- **Income stability and documentation.** Consistent, well-documented income gets better terms.
- **Credit history.** Late payments or high card utilisation raise the rate or reduce the amount.
- **End use.** Some lenders price business-use LAP differently from personal use.
- **Tenure.** Longer tenure lowers the EMI but increases total interest.

## The Process

1. **Eligibility check** based on income and a rough property value.
2. **Application and documents**, including the full property file.
3. **Legal and technical verification.** The lender's lawyer checks the title; the valuer inspects the property.
4. **Sanction**, with the final amount, rate, tenure and charges in writing.
5. **Mortgage creation and disbursal.** The original documents are deposited with the lender and the loan is paid out.

Read the sanction letter carefully for processing fees, legal and valuation charges, and any part-prepayment or foreclosure terms before you sign.
