## Before You Begin

Applying for a loan is straightforward once you understand the sequence. Rushing in without preparation is the most common reason applications get rejected or land you a worse rate. This tutorial walks you through the entire process, from deciding how much to borrow to receiving the money in your account. Follow the steps in order and you will apply once, cleanly, instead of scattering rejected applications that hurt your credit score.

## Step 1: Define Your Need and Amount

Start by answering two questions honestly: why do you need the loan, and exactly how much do you need? Borrow for a clear purpose, and borrow only what that purpose requires. Over-borrowing inflates your EMI and interest cost for years.

Match the purpose to the right product:

- Wedding, medical or travel: [Personal Loan](/loans/personal-loan)
- Buying or building a house: [Home Loan](/loans/home-loan)
- Business expansion or working capital: [Business Loan](/loans/business-loan)
- Large sum against owned property: [Loan Against Property](/loans/loan-against-property)

## Step 2: Check Your Credit Score

Your credit score is the first thing a lender looks at. A [CIBIL score](/blog/what-is-a-good-credit-score-in-india) of 750 or above puts you in the strongest position for approval and the best rates. Pull your free score before applying so there are no surprises.

If your score is below 700, consider delaying your application by a few months while you improve it: pay down credit-card balances, clear any overdue amounts and avoid new credit enquiries. A higher score can save you thousands in interest. Learn how to interpret your report in our [how to read your credit report](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-read-your-credit-report) tutorial.

## Step 3: Confirm Your Eligibility

Every lender publishes eligibility criteria covering age, income, employment and credit score. Before applying, confirm you meet them to avoid a wasted hard enquiry. Our [how to check loan eligibility](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-check-loan-eligibility) tutorial explains exactly how to assess yourself against each lender's requirements, and many lenders offer a soft pre-check that does not affect your score.

## Step 4: Compare Lenders and Offers

Do not apply to the first lender you find. Compare across several on four dimensions:

- **Interest rate** (always the reducing-balance rate and the APR)
- **Processing fee** and other charges
- **Prepayment / foreclosure terms**
- **Disbursal speed** and customer service

A low headline rate paired with a high processing fee can cost more than a slightly higher rate with no fee. Browse our current [offers](/offers) and use the [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator) to compare the real monthly and total cost of each option.

## Step 5: Choose Your Loan Amount and Tenure

Once you have picked a lender, decide your tenure. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises total interest; a shorter tenure does the opposite. The right choice keeps your EMI comfortably below 40 percent of your take-home pay while minimising total interest. Run a few scenarios on the [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator) and pick the shortest tenure whose EMI still leaves you breathing room.

## Step 6: Gather Your Documents

Having documents ready speeds up approval dramatically. The core set for most loans is:

- **Identity and address:** PAN card and Aadhaar
- **Income (salaried):** Last 3 months salary slips, Form 16
- **Income (self-employed):** Last 2-3 years ITRs and financials
- **Bank statements:** Last 6-12 months
- **Photographs:** Passport-size
- **Property papers:** For home loans and LAP, the full property document set

Keep clear scans or photos ready. Incomplete or blurry documents are a leading cause of delay.

## Step 7: Submit Your Application

You can apply online or at a branch. The online route is usually faster:

1. Fill the application form accurately. Even small mismatches between your form and your documents cause delays.
2. Upload your documents.
3. Complete KYC verification, often via Aadhaar-based e-KYC.
4. Authenticate income, sometimes through account aggregator or bank-statement upload.
5. Submit and note your application reference number.

Apply to only one or two best-fit lenders. Each formal application triggers a hard enquiry, and several in a short window lower your score.

## Step 8: Verification and Underwriting

After submission, the lender verifies your details, runs a credit check and assesses your repayment capacity via your FOIR. For secured loans, a valuation and legal check of the collateral follows. Respond promptly to any request for additional documents; delays here are usually the applicant's, not the lender's.

## Step 9: Review the Sanction Letter

If approved, the lender issues a sanction letter stating the loan amount, interest rate, tenure, EMI, processing fee and all charges. Read every line. This is your moment to confirm the terms match what you were promised and to spot any bundled insurance or hidden fee. If anything differs from the offer, question it before signing.

## Step 10: Accept, Sign and Disburse

Once you accept the terms, you sign the loan agreement and set up repayment, typically a NACH mandate that auto-debits your EMI each month. The lender then disburses the funds: to your account for a personal or business loan, or to the seller or builder for a home loan. Note your first EMI date and ensure your account holds sufficient balance to avoid a bounce.

## After Disbursal: Repay Well

Your job now is timely repayment. Set a reminder a few days before each EMI, keep your account funded and never miss a payment; even one late EMI dents your credit score. As your income grows, consider prepaying to save interest. If a cheaper offer appears later, a [balance transfer](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-do-a-balance-transfer) can reduce your cost.

## Quick Checklist

- Define purpose and amount
- Check your [credit score](/blog/what-is-a-good-credit-score-in-india)
- Confirm eligibility
- Compare lenders and [offers](/offers)
- Choose amount and tenure via the [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator)
- Gather documents
- Apply to one or two lenders
- Review the sanction letter carefully
- Sign, set up NACH and receive funds
- Repay on time, every time
