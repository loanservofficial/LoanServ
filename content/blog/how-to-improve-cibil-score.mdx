## Why Your CIBIL Score Decides Your Loan Cost

In India, your CIBIL score is the single number most lenders check before approving a personal loan, home loan or credit card. It ranges from 300 to 900, and anything above 750 usually unlocks the best interest rates. A weak score does not just risk rejection, it quietly costs you lakhs over the life of a loan because banks price you higher.

The good news: your score is not fixed. With disciplined habits, most borrowers in Hyderabad, Bangalore, Chennai and across AP and Telangana can see meaningful improvement within three to six months. This guide breaks down exactly what to do.

## Understand What Goes Into the Score

CIBIL (TransUnion CIBIL) and the other three bureaus, Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark, calculate scores using broadly similar factors:

| Factor | Approximate weight | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment history | 35% | Whether you pay EMIs and card bills on time |
| Credit utilisation | 30% | How much of your credit limit you use |
| Credit age and mix | 15% | Length and variety of your credit |
| New enquiries | 10% | How often you apply for fresh credit |
| Other factors | 10% | Outstanding balances, recent activity |

Once you know the weights, the priorities become obvious. Payment history and utilisation together drive nearly two-thirds of your score.

## Step 1: Pull Your Credit Report and Fix Errors

Before changing habits, see what lenders see. You are entitled to one free report a year from each bureau, and you can check yours on any bureau's website. Read every line and look for:

- Loans or cards you never opened (possible identity theft)
- Accounts wrongly marked as overdue or defaulted
- A closed loan still showing as active
- Incorrect personal details or duplicate accounts

If you spot an error, raise a dispute with the bureau directly. Corrections can lift a score by 20 to 50 points once resolved, and they cost nothing but a little patience.

## Step 2: Never Miss a Payment

A single 30-day late payment can pull your score down by 50 to 80 points and stays on your report for years. Automate everything:

1. Set up auto-debit or standing instructions for every EMI and credit card bill.
2. Keep enough balance in your account two days before the due date.
3. If money is tight, at least pay the minimum due to avoid a "missed payment" flag.

Consistency matters more than the amount. Twelve months of on-time payments is the strongest signal you can send a lender.

## Step 3: Keep Credit Utilisation Below 30%

Credit utilisation is your outstanding card balance divided by your total limit. If you have a ₹1,00,000 limit and carry ₹60,000, your utilisation is 60%, which bureaus read as financial stress.

Ways to bring it down:

- **Pay early or twice a month** so the balance reported to the bureau is low.
- **Request a limit increase** from your bank if your income has grown; a higher limit lowers utilisation instantly.
- **Spread spends** across two cards rather than maxing out one.

Aim for under 30%, and under 10% if you want an excellent score.

## Step 4: Be Strategic About New Credit

Every time you apply for a loan or card, the lender runs a "hard enquiry" that shaves a few points and stays visible for months. Applying to five lenders in a week looks desperate to an algorithm.

Instead, compare offers first and apply only where you are likely to qualify. When shopping for a [personal loan](/loans/personal-loan), use eligibility checkers that run a soft enquiry, which does not affect your score.

## Step 5: Build a Healthy Credit Mix and Age

Lenders like to see that you can handle both secured credit (home loan, car loan) and unsecured credit (personal loan, credit card). If your only history is one card, adding a small, well-managed loan over time can help.

Also, do not rush to close your oldest credit card. A card you have held for eight years lengthens your average credit age, which is a positive factor. Keep it active with a small recurring spend.

## Step 6: Clear Old Defaults and Settlements

If your report shows a "settled" or "written off" account, that is a red flag to future lenders. Where possible:

- Negotiate to pay the full outstanding rather than a settlement, and ask the lender to report the account as "closed".
- Get a No Dues Certificate in writing.
- Wait for the updated status to reflect, then verify on your report.

Converting a settled account to fully paid can meaningfully improve how lenders view you.

## How Long Does Improvement Take?

There is no overnight fix, but here is a realistic timeline:

- **1 to 2 months:** Report errors corrected, utilisation drops.
- **3 to 6 months:** On-time payment streak starts showing benefits.
- **6 to 12 months:** Old negatives age, score stabilises in a higher band.

Borrowers who start in the 650s often reach 750-plus within a year of disciplined behaviour.

## A Quick Worked Example

Suppose Priya in Hyderabad has a score of 690. Her report shows one card at 80% utilisation and a duplicate loan entry. She:

1. Disputes the duplicate (removed in 40 days).
2. Pays down the card to 25% utilisation.
3. Sets auto-pay on all bills.

Six months later her score is 758, and she qualifies for a personal loan at 11.5% instead of the 16% she was quoted earlier, saving thousands in interest. Try our [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator) to see how a lower rate changes your monthly outgo.

## Key Takeaways

- Payment history and credit utilisation together drive nearly 65% of your CIBIL score, so fix these first.
- Check your report at least once a year and dispute errors, which is free and often adds points quickly.
- Keep card utilisation under 30%, automate every payment, and avoid clustering loan applications.
- Do not close your oldest card, and convert any settled accounts to fully paid.
- Expect real improvement in three to six months of consistent behaviour; there are no legitimate shortcuts.
