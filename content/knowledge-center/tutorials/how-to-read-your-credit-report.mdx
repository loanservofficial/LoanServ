## Why Your Credit Report Matters

Your credit report is the single most important document a lender sees before approving your loan. It is a detailed record of how you have borrowed and repaid over the years, and it drives both whether you get approved and what interest rate you are offered. Yet most borrowers have never read theirs carefully. This tutorial teaches you to read every section of your report, understand what shapes your score and spot errors that could be quietly costing you money.

In India, credit reports are maintained by four bureaus: CIBIL (TransUnion CIBIL), Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark. You are entitled to one free full report from each bureau every year. Grab your score anytime on any bureau's website.

## Step 1: Obtain Your Credit Report

Start by getting your full report, not just the score. Visit a bureau's website, complete identity verification, and download the report. Reading it once a year, or before any major loan application, lets you fix problems before a lender sees them.

## Step 2: Understand the Credit Score

At the top sits your three-digit score, ranging from 300 to 900. Here is how to read it:

| Score | Rating | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 750 to 900 | Excellent | Best rates, easy approval |
| 700 to 749 | Good | Likely approval |
| 650 to 699 | Fair | Approval possible, higher rates |
| 300 to 649 | Poor | Approval difficult |

The score is a summary; the sections below explain why it is what it is.

## Step 3: Verify Your Personal Information

The first section lists your personal details: name, date of birth, PAN, Aadhaar, address, phone and email. Check every field.

- An incorrect PAN or name can cause your accounts to be mixed with someone else's.
- An unfamiliar address may signal that another person's data has merged into your file.
- Outdated contact details can mean you miss important lender communication.

Any error here should be flagged and disputed, because it can distort your entire report.

## Step 4: Review Your Account Information

This is the heart of the report. It lists every credit account you hold or have held: loans and credit cards, with the lender name, account type, sanctioned amount or credit limit, current balance, and, crucially, the repayment history.

For each account, examine:

- **Account status:** Active, closed or settled. A "settled" or "written off" status is a red flag that lowers your score.
- **Payment history:** A month-by-month record showing whether each EMI or bill was paid on time. "000" or "STD" means on-time; higher numbers (030, 060, 090) show days past due.
- **Current balance and overdue amount:** Any overdue amount drags your score down and must be cleared.
- **Ownership:** Whether you are the borrower, co-applicant or guarantor. You are liable even as a guarantor.

Scan for accounts you do not recognise; they could indicate fraud or a bureau error.

## Step 5: Understand Credit Utilisation

For credit cards and revolving credit, note how much of your limit you are using, your credit utilisation ratio. Keeping utilisation below 30 percent of your total limit signals healthy credit behaviour and supports a strong score. If your report shows high utilisation, paying down balances is one of the fastest ways to lift your score before a loan application.

## Step 6: Check the Enquiry Section

Every time you formally apply for credit, the lender makes a hard enquiry, and it is recorded here. A cluster of enquiries in a short period signals credit hunger and lowers your score. Review this section for:

- **Enquiries you made** and remember, which are normal.
- **Enquiries you do not recognise,** which may indicate someone applied for credit in your name, a possible sign of identity theft.

This is why you should compare lenders first and apply to only one or two, as covered in our [how to apply for a loan](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-apply-for-a-loan) tutorial.

## Step 7: Know What Shapes Your Score

Understanding the drivers helps you improve. The main factors, roughly in order of weight, are:

- **Payment history:** Paying every EMI and bill on time is the biggest driver. A single default can hurt for months.
- **Credit utilisation:** Lower is better; stay under 30 percent.
- **Credit age and mix:** A longer history and a healthy mix of secured and unsecured credit help.
- **New credit and enquiries:** Frequent applications hurt.

Managing these deliberately is how you move from a fair score to an excellent one.

## Step 8: Spot and Dispute Errors

Errors are more common than people assume, and they can cost you a loan or a better rate. Watch for:

- A loan or card you closed still shown as active or with an outstanding balance
- A payment you made on time recorded as late
- An account or enquiry that is not yours
- A wrongly reported "settled" or "written off" status
- Incorrect personal details

To dispute an error, raise a request on the bureau's website with supporting evidence. The bureau investigates with the lender, and if your claim is valid, the record is corrected, usually within 30 days. Correcting a genuine error can lift your score noticeably.

## Step 9: Build a Habit of Monitoring

Reading your report once and forgetting it is a mistake. Check it at least once a year, and always before applying for a major loan, so you can fix issues in advance. Regular monitoring also catches fraud early, before it damages your ability to borrow. A bureau's own score check is free and counts as a soft enquiry, so use it to keep an eye on your score between full reports.

## Turn a Good Report into Better Loans

A clean, well-understood credit report is your strongest asset when borrowing. A score above 750 and an error-free history put you in the best position to negotiate rates on a [personal loan](/loans/personal-loan), [home loan](/loans/home-loan), [business loan](/loans/business-loan) or [loan against property](/loans/loan-against-property).

## Credit Report Checklist

- Downloaded your full report, not just the score
- Verified all personal details
- Reviewed every account's status and payment history
- Checked credit utilisation is under 30 percent
- Reviewed the enquiry section for unfamiliar entries
- Disputed any errors with evidence
- Set a reminder to monitor at least annually

Master your credit report and every future loan becomes cheaper and easier.
