## What Is a Personal Loan?

A personal loan is an unsecured loan that you can use for almost any legitimate purpose: a wedding, medical emergency, home renovation, travel, debt consolidation or a big-ticket purchase. Because it is unsecured, you do not pledge any collateral such as property or gold. Lenders instead rely on your income, employment stability and credit history to decide whether to approve your application and at what interest rate.

In India, personal loans are offered by banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and digital lending platforms. Loan amounts typically range from about Rs. 50,000 to Rs. 40 lakh, and repayment tenures usually fall between 12 and 60 months. The flexibility, quick disbursal and minimal documentation make personal loans one of the most popular retail credit products in the country.

## Who Is Eligible for a Personal Loan?

While every lender sets its own rules, most eligibility criteria in India revolve around a few common factors.

- **Age:** Usually between 21 and 60 years for salaried applicants and up to 65 years for self-employed applicants.
- **Income:** Salaried applicants often need a minimum net monthly income of Rs. 15,000 to Rs. 25,000, though metro-city lenders may ask for more.
- **Employment:** Salaried applicants generally need at least 6 to 12 months in the current job; self-employed applicants need 2 to 3 years of business continuity.
- **Credit score:** A [CIBIL score](/blog/what-is-a-good-credit-score-in-india) of 750 or above significantly improves approval odds and helps you negotiate better rates.
- **Debt-to-income ratio:** Lenders check your FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio) to ensure your existing EMIs plus the new EMI do not exceed roughly 40-50 percent of your income.

You can estimate your chances before applying by reading our tutorial on [how to check loan eligibility](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-check-loan-eligibility).

## Personal Loan Interest Rates in India

Personal loan interest rates typically range from around 10.5 percent to 24 percent per annum. Your exact rate depends on your credit profile, income, employer category, loan amount and relationship with the lender.

Two rate structures matter:

- **Reducing balance rate:** Interest is charged only on the outstanding principal, which shrinks with every EMI. This is the standard and fairer method.
- **Flat rate:** Interest is charged on the full original principal for the entire tenure. A flat rate always works out more expensive than an equivalent-sounding reducing rate, so always ask lenders to quote the reducing-balance figure and the APR.

Use our [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator) to see how different rates and tenures change your monthly outgo before you commit.

## Fees and Charges to Watch

The advertised interest rate is only part of the story. Read the sanction letter carefully for these charges:

| Charge | Typical Range |
| --- | --- |
| Processing fee | 0.5% to 3% of loan amount |
| Prepayment/foreclosure fee | 0% to 5% of outstanding |
| Late payment penalty | 2% to 3% per month on overdue EMI |
| Cheque/NACH bounce charge | Rs. 300 to Rs. 750 per instance |
| GST | 18% on fees and charges |

Some lenders now offer zero-foreclosure charges on floating-rate loans. If you expect to prepay early, prioritise these lenders.

## Documents You Will Need

Personal loans are known for light documentation, but keep these ready to speed up approval.

**For salaried applicants:**

- PAN card and Aadhaar (identity and address proof)
- Latest 3 months salary slips
- Last 6 months bank statements showing salary credit
- Employment proof or offer letter

**For self-employed applicants:**

- PAN and Aadhaar
- Last 2-3 years income tax returns with computation
- Business proof (GST registration, shop licence or partnership deed)
- Last 6-12 months bank statements

## How to Plan Your EMI Sensibly

The single most common borrowing mistake is fixing on the loan amount you can get rather than the EMI you can comfortably repay. A prudent rule is to keep all EMIs combined below 40 percent of your monthly take-home pay.

Consider a Rs. 5 lakh loan at 14 percent for different tenures:

- **24 months:** Higher EMI, but you pay far less total interest.
- **48 months:** Lower EMI, but total interest nearly doubles.

Longer tenures feel comfortable each month but quietly inflate your total cost. Pick the shortest tenure whose EMI still leaves you breathing room for savings and emergencies. Model a few scenarios on our [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator) first.

## Using a Personal Loan to Consolidate Debt

If you are juggling multiple credit-card balances at 36-42 percent annual interest, a personal loan at 12-16 percent can consolidate them into one cheaper EMI. This lowers your interest cost, simplifies repayment and can improve your credit score by reducing credit-card utilisation. Just make sure you do not run the cards back up after clearing them, or you will end up worse off with two debts instead of one.

## Prepayment and Foreclosure

Prepaying part of your loan when you receive a bonus or windfall reduces your outstanding principal and total interest. Foreclosure means closing the loan entirely before the tenure ends. Check three things first: any foreclosure charge, the minimum lock-in period before prepayment is allowed, and whether the savings justify the fee. For a deeper walkthrough, see our tutorial on [how to do a balance transfer](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-do-a-balance-transfer), which often pairs with prepayment strategies.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Applying to many lenders at once:** Each application triggers a hard enquiry that can lower your score. Compare first, then apply to one or two best-fit lenders.
- **Ignoring the APR:** A low headline rate with a high processing fee can cost more than a slightly higher rate with no fee.
- **Borrowing for depreciating wants:** Reserve personal loans for genuine needs or cost-saving consolidation, not lifestyle spending you cannot otherwise afford.
- **Not reading the fine print:** Insurance bundled with the loan, mandatory add-ons and hidden charges can inflate your cost.

## Improve Your Approval Odds

Before applying, check your credit report for errors and settle any small overdue amounts. A clean report and a score above 750 put you in the strongest bargaining position.
