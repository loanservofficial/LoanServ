## Funding Higher Education Without Draining Your Savings

Higher education in India and abroad has become expensive. An engineering degree, an MBA or a master's overseas can run into tens of lakhs. An education loan lets bright students pursue their goals without their families liquidating savings or property. This guide covers everything an Indian student and parent needs to know before signing up.

Education loans are among the most borrower-friendly products available, with long moratoriums, tax benefits and, in many cases, no collateral for smaller amounts.

## What an Education Loan Covers

A typical education loan finances far more than just tuition:

- Tuition and college fees
- Examination, library and laboratory fees
- Hostel and accommodation charges
- Books, equipment and, for some courses, a laptop
- Travel expenses for studying abroad
- Caution deposits and refundable charges

Lenders usually require the family to contribute a **margin** — often around 5% for domestic courses and 15% for foreign courses on loans above a threshold. Smaller loans may have zero margin.

## Eligibility Criteria

To qualify, most lenders require:

- The **student** to be an Indian citizen with a confirmed admission to a recognised institution.
- A **co-applicant** — usually a parent or guardian — whose income and credit profile anchor the loan.
- Admission secured through merit or an entrance exam to a course with reasonable employability.

The co-applicant's **CIBIL score** and income are crucial, since the student typically has no independent credit history. Check the co-applicant's score in advance on any bureau's website.

## Collateral: Secured vs Unsecured

Collateral requirements usually scale with the loan amount. Indicative thresholds:

| Loan Amount | Collateral Requirement |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4 lakh | No collateral, no margin |
| ₹4 lakh - ₹7.5 lakh | Third-party guarantee often needed |
| Above ₹7.5 lakh | Tangible collateral usually required |

Figures are indicative and vary by lender and scheme. Collateral can be property, fixed deposits or securities. For premier institutions (IITs, IIMs, top global universities), many lenders offer larger unsecured loans based on the course's strong repayment track record.

## The Moratorium Period

A defining feature of education loans is the **moratorium** or repayment holiday. You typically do not have to start full EMIs during your course, plus an additional grace period of **6 to 12 months** after completion (or after getting a job, in some schemes).

This lets you begin repaying only once you are earning. Note two things:

- **Interest usually accrues during the moratorium.** Even if you pay nothing, interest is charged and added to your principal.
- **Servicing simple interest during the study period** — if you can afford it — meaningfully reduces your total cost, and some lenders offer a small rate concession for doing so.

## Interest Rates and Subsidies

Education loan rates in India generally range from around 8.5% to 14%, depending on the lender, course, institution and collateral. Female students often receive a small concession of around 0.5%.

Several **government interest subsidy schemes** exist for eligible students, particularly from economically weaker sections, where the government pays the interest during the moratorium for approved courses. Eligibility conditions and income ceilings apply, so check current scheme rules before assuming a subsidy.

## Tax Benefit Under Section 80E

A major advantage: the **interest** paid on an education loan is fully deductible under **Section 80E** of the Income Tax Act, with **no upper limit** on the amount. The deduction is available for up to **8 years** from the year repayment begins, or until the interest is fully repaid, whichever is earlier.

Note that this benefit applies under the old tax regime and covers interest only, not principal. It significantly lowers the effective cost of borrowing for salaried graduates.

## Studying Abroad: Extra Considerations

If you are heading overseas, factor in:

- **Larger loan amounts** and stricter collateral requirements.
- **Forex fluctuations** — a weaker rupee raises your effective cost.
- **Country and course selection** — lenders favour STEM and professional courses with strong job prospects.
- **Proof of admission and visa** documentation.

Some borrowers use a mix of an education loan and a top-up from a [Home Loan](/loans/home-loan) against family property when large sums are needed. Compare total costs carefully before combining products.

## Documents You Will Need

- Admission letter and fee schedule from the institution
- KYC of student and co-applicant (PAN, Aadhaar)
- Academic records (marksheets, entrance exam scores)
- Income proof of co-applicant (salary slips / ITR)
- Collateral documents, if applicable
- Bank statements of the co-applicant

## Tips to Get the Best Deal

1. **Compare public-sector banks, private banks and NBFCs.** Public banks often offer lower rates; NBFCs may be faster and more flexible for study abroad.
2. **Choose a strong co-applicant** with a clean, high CIBIL score.
3. **Service interest during the moratorium** if you can, to cut total cost.
4. **Prefer floating rates** if you expect rates to fall, and remember individual floating loans have no prepayment penalty.
5. **Keep documentation complete and honest** to avoid delays.

## Key Takeaways

- Education loans cover tuition, living costs, travel and equipment, with a small family margin.
- Loans up to ₹4 lakh usually need no collateral; larger amounts often require security.
- A moratorium lets you defer EMIs during study, but interest still accrues — service it if you can.
- Section 80E allows unlimited interest deduction for up to 8 years under the old regime.
- The co-applicant's income and CIBIL score are central to approval.
- Compare banks and NBFCs, and plan your post-study EMI before you borrow.
