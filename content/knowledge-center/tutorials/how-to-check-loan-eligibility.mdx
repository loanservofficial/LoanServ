## Why Check Eligibility First

Checking your eligibility before you apply is the single smartest thing you can do as a borrower. It tells you whether you qualify, how much you can borrow and roughly what rate to expect, all without triggering a hard enquiry that lowers your credit score. This tutorial shows you exactly how to assess yourself against the criteria that Indian lenders use, so that when you do apply, you apply once and get approved.

## Understand the Five Pillars of Eligibility

Almost every lender in India evaluates a loan application on five pillars. Assess yourself honestly against each.

- **Credit score** — your track record of repaying past debt
- **Income** — your capacity to service the new EMI
- **Existing obligations (FOIR)** — how much of your income is already committed
- **Age and employment stability** — the reliability of your income over the tenure
- **Collateral** — for secured loans, the value and clarity of the pledged asset

Work through each pillar below.

## Step 1: Check Your Credit Score

Start here, because a weak score can override everything else. Pull your [credit report](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-read-your-credit-report) and note where you stand:

| Score Range | What It Means |
| --- | --- |
| 750 and above | Excellent; best rates and easy approval |
| 700 to 749 | Good; likely approval, decent rates |
| 650 to 699 | Fair; approval possible, higher rates |
| Below 650 | Poor; approval difficult, work on it first |

If your score is below 700, spend a few months improving it before applying. Pay down credit-card balances, clear overdue amounts and avoid new enquiries. Understand your report fully with our [how to read your credit report](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-read-your-credit-report) tutorial.

## Step 2: Assess Your Income

Lenders set a minimum income threshold that varies by product, city and lender. Salaried applicants often need a net monthly income of Rs. 15,000 to Rs. 25,000 or more; self-employed applicants are judged on annual profit and turnover from their ITRs.

Note your net monthly take-home income (after tax and deductions), since lenders use this, not your gross salary, to size your loan. Higher, stable and formally documented income raises both your approval odds and your eligible amount.

## Step 3: Calculate Your FOIR

FOIR, the Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio, is how lenders check whether you can afford a new EMI on top of your existing commitments. The formula is simple:

**FOIR = (Total existing EMIs + proposed new EMI) / Net monthly income**

Most lenders want your FOIR to stay below 40-50 percent. Here is how to compute it:

1. Add up all your current monthly EMIs and fixed obligations (loans, credit-card minimums, rent where counted).
2. Estimate the EMI of the new loan using our [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator).
3. Add the two, then divide by your net monthly income.

If the result exceeds 50 percent, you are unlikely to qualify for the full amount. Reduce the loan amount, extend the tenure to lower the EMI, or clear an existing debt first.

## Step 4: Check Age and Employment Criteria

Lenders want the loan fully repaid within your earning years.

- **Age:** Usually 21 to 60 for salaried and up to 65-70 for self-employed, with the loan maturing before an upper age limit.
- **Employment stability:** Salaried applicants generally need 6-12 months in the current job and some total work experience; self-employed applicants need 2-3 years of business continuity.

Job-hopping or a very new business can hold up approval even with good income, so factor this in.

## Step 5: Evaluate Collateral (for Secured Loans)

For a [home loan](/loans/home-loan) or [loan against property](/loans/loan-against-property), the collateral matters as much as your profile. Assess:

- **Clear title:** The property must be free of disputes and legally approved.
- **Market value:** Determines the loan amount via the LTV ratio.
- **Marketability:** Well-located, standard properties fetch higher LTV than remote or unusual ones.

For unsecured loans like a [personal loan](/loans/personal-loan) or unsecured [business loan](/loans/business-loan), skip this pillar; your profile alone decides the outcome.

## Step 6: Use Online Eligibility Tools

Most lenders offer an online eligibility checker or pre-approved offer engine. These use a soft enquiry that does not affect your score, and they give you a realistic sense of your eligible amount and rate. Enter your income, obligations and employment details honestly to get a useful estimate. Combine this with our [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator) to confirm the resulting EMI fits your budget.

## Step 7: Match Yourself to the Right Lender

Different lenders weight the five pillars differently. Some prioritise credit score, others income or employer category. If your score is strong but income modest, seek lenders who reward creditworthiness. If your income is high but score recovering, look for lenders who weigh repayment capacity more heavily. ## Ways to Improve Your Eligibility

If you fall short, you have several levers:

- **Add a co-applicant:** An earning spouse or parent raises the combined income and eligible amount.
- **Extend the tenure:** A longer tenure lowers the EMI and improves FOIR, though it raises total interest.
- **Reduce existing debt:** Closing a small loan or credit-card balance improves your FOIR immediately.
- **Boost your credit score:** A few months of clean repayment can move you into a better bracket.
- **Declare all income:** Include verifiable additional income such as rent or a second job.

## Eligibility Checklist

- Pulled your [credit score](/blog/what-is-a-good-credit-score-in-india) and know your bracket
- Noted your net monthly income
- Calculated your FOIR and confirmed it is below 50 percent
- Confirmed you meet age and employment criteria
- Assessed collateral (for secured loans)
- Used an online eligibility tool for a realistic estimate
- Matched yourself to lenders whose criteria fit
