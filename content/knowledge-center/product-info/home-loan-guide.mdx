## Understanding Home Loans in India

A home loan is a secured loan used to buy, construct, renovate or extend a residential property. The property itself acts as collateral, which means the lender holds a charge on it until you repay in full. Because the loan is secured, interest rates are far lower than unsecured products, and tenures stretch up to 30 years, making home ownership affordable through manageable monthly instalments.

Home loans in India are offered by banks, housing finance companies (HFCs) and NBFCs. A typical loan finances 75-90 percent of the property value, with you contributing the rest as a down payment. Given the size and length of the commitment, a home loan deserves more research than any other borrowing decision you will make. ## Loan-to-Value Ratio and Down Payment

Lenders do not finance the entire cost of a property. The Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio caps how much they lend against the property value:

| Property Value | Maximum LTV |
| --- | --- |
| Up to Rs. 30 lakh | Up to 90% |
| Rs. 30 lakh to Rs. 75 lakh | Up to 80% |
| Above Rs. 75 lakh | Up to 75% |

The balance is your down payment, funded from savings. A larger down payment reduces your loan amount, lowers your EMI and total interest, and signals financial discipline to the lender. Aim for at least 20 percent even when a lender offers more, plus a buffer for stamp duty, registration and other costs that the loan does not cover.

## Eligibility Criteria

Home loan eligibility hinges on your ability to repay a long-tenure loan.

- **Age:** Typically 21 to 65 years, with the loan maturing before you turn 70.
- **Income:** Stable, verifiable income is essential. Lenders assess your FOIR to ensure EMIs stay within about 40-55 percent of net income.
- **Credit score:** A [CIBIL score](/blog/what-is-a-good-credit-score-in-india) above 750 unlocks the best rates and higher sanction amounts.
- **Employment:** Salaried applicants need job stability; self-employed applicants need 3 or more years of business continuity and consistent ITRs.
- **Property:** The property must have clear title and approved plans, since the lender is lending against it.

Adding a co-applicant, especially an earning spouse, can boost your eligible loan amount and may unlock a lower rate for women co-owners. Check your standing first with our [how to check loan eligibility](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-check-loan-eligibility) tutorial.

## Fixed vs Floating Interest Rates

Home loans come with two rate structures, and the choice materially affects your cost.

- **Floating rate:** Linked to an external benchmark such as the RBI repo rate. Your EMI or tenure adjusts as the benchmark moves. Floating rates are usually lower to start and carry no foreclosure charges on individual borrowers.
- **Fixed rate:** Stays constant for a defined period, giving predictability but usually at a higher starting rate. Pure long-term fixed rates are rare in India.

Most borrowers choose floating rates for their lower cost and prepayment flexibility. If you expect interest rates to fall or plan to prepay aggressively, floating is generally the smarter pick. Model both on our [home loan EMI calculator](/calculators/emi-calculator).

## Home Loan Interest Rates

Home loan rates in India generally range from about 8.3 percent to 11 percent per annum, depending on your profile, the lender and the benchmark. Even a 0.25 percent difference on a Rs. 50 lakh, 20-year loan translates into lakhs of rupees over the tenure, so negotiating your rate is worth the effort. Existing customers, women borrowers and those with strong credit scores often qualify for concessions.

## Tax Benefits on Home Loans

One of the biggest advantages of a home loan is the tax relief under the old tax regime:

- **Section 80C:** Deduction of up to Rs. 1.5 lakh per year on principal repayment.
- **Section 24(b):** Deduction of up to Rs. 2 lakh per year on interest paid for a self-occupied property.
- **Section 80EEA:** Additional interest deduction for eligible first-time buyers on affordable housing, subject to conditions.
- **Stamp duty and registration:** Claimable under Section 80C in the year paid, within the overall limit.

These benefits can significantly reduce your effective cost of borrowing. Confirm the current rules and whether you are on the old or new regime with a tax advisor, since deductions largely apply under the old regime.

## Documents Required

- PAN, Aadhaar and passport-size photographs
- Income proof: salary slips and Form 16 for salaried; ITRs and financials for self-employed
- Last 6-12 months bank statements
- Property documents: sale agreement, title deed, approved building plan, and NOC where applicable
- Proof of down payment or own contribution

## Repaying Smart: Prepayment and Tenure

Because home loans run for decades, small actions early save large sums. In the initial years, most of your EMI goes toward interest, so prepaying early has an outsized impact.

- **Prepay lump sums** from bonuses or maturing investments to cut the principal directly.
- **Increase your EMI** slightly each year as your income grows to shorten the tenure.
- **Review your rate** periodically. If your lender's rate lags the market, ask for a reset or consider a [balance transfer](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-do-a-balance-transfer) to a cheaper lender.

Since floating-rate home loans carry no prepayment penalty for individuals, aggressive prepayment is one of the most powerful wealth-building moves available to a homeowner.

## Balance Transfer and Top-Up

If a rival lender offers a materially lower rate, transferring your outstanding loan can reduce your EMI or tenure. Weigh the processing fee and legal costs against the interest savings; a transfer usually makes sense early in the tenure when the outstanding is large. Many lenders also offer a top-up loan alongside the transfer at home-loan rates, useful for renovation or other needs at a far lower cost than a personal loan.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Underestimating total cost:** Stamp duty, registration, GST on under-construction property, brokerage and interiors add 10-15 percent beyond the sticker price.
- **Stretching the EMI:** A comfortable EMI leaves room for emergencies. Do not max out your eligibility.
- **Skipping the fine print:** Watch for mandatory insurance, conversion fees and MODT charges.
- **Ignoring your credit report:** Errors can raise your rate. Learn to spot them with our [how to read your credit report](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-read-your-credit-report) guide.
