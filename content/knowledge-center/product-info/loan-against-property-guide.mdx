## What Is a Loan Against Property?

A Loan Against Property (LAP) is a secured loan where you pledge a residential, commercial or industrial property you own as collateral to borrow a large sum of money. Because the loan is backed by real estate, lenders offer lower interest rates, larger amounts and longer tenures than any unsecured product. Crucially, you retain ownership and continued use of the property; you simply grant the lender a charge on it until the loan is repaid.

LAP is a versatile, general-purpose loan. The funds can be used for business expansion, funding a child's higher education abroad, a medical emergency, debt consolidation or a wedding. This flexibility, combined with a low cost, makes LAP one of the most powerful borrowing tools for anyone who owns property. ## How LAP Works

You approach a lender with property you own. The lender values the property, assesses your repayment capacity, and sanctions a loan as a percentage of the property's market value, the Loan-to-Value ratio. You repay through EMIs over a tenure that can extend to 15 years or more. If you default, the lender has the legal right to sell the property to recover its dues, which is why interest rates are low but the stakes are high.

## Loan-to-Value Ratio

Lenders typically sanction 50 to 70 percent of the property's assessed market value under LAP, lower than a home loan because the funds are used for general purposes rather than the property itself.

| Property Type | Typical LTV |
| --- | --- |
| Self-occupied residential | 60% to 70% |
| Rented / commercial | 50% to 65% |
| Industrial / plots | 40% to 55% |

The exact percentage depends on the property type, location, marketability and your repayment profile. A well-located, clearly titled residential property fetches the highest LTV.

## Eligibility Criteria

- **Ownership:** You must own the property with a clear, marketable title. Co-owned properties usually require all owners to be co-applicants.
- **Age:** Generally 21 to 65-70 years at loan maturity.
- **Income:** Both salaried and self-employed applicants qualify. Stable, verifiable income is essential since the tenure is long.
- **Credit score:** A [CIBIL score](/blog/what-is-a-good-credit-score-in-india) above 700-750 improves your rate and sanction amount.
- **Property condition:** The property should be legally approved, free of disputes and not too old, so the lender can value and, if necessary, liquidate it.

## Interest Rates

LAP interest rates in India generally range from about 9 percent to 14 percent per annum, sitting between home loans and unsecured personal or business loans. Rates are usually floating and linked to an external benchmark. Your specific rate depends on the property type, loan amount, your income profile and credit score. Because the tenure is long and the amount large, negotiating even a small reduction in rate saves a substantial sum. ## Fees and Charges

| Charge | Typical Range |
| --- | --- |
| Processing fee | 0.5% to 2% of loan amount |
| Legal and valuation charges | Rs. 5,000 to Rs. 25,000+ |
| Prepayment / foreclosure | 0% to 4% (often nil on floating for individuals) |
| Penal interest | 2% to 3% per month on overdue |
| GST | 18% on fees |

Factor these into your total cost when comparing lenders.

## Documents Required

- KYC: PAN, Aadhaar, address proof and photographs
- Income proof: salary slips and Form 16 for salaried; ITRs and financials for self-employed
- Last 6-12 months bank statements
- Complete property documents: title deed, chain of ownership, approved plan, latest tax receipts and encumbrance certificate

Clear, complete property papers are the single biggest factor in a smooth, fast LAP approval.

## Smart Uses of LAP

LAP shines when you need a large sum at a low cost and can service a long-tenure EMI comfortably:

- **Business expansion:** Cheaper than an unsecured business loan for large capital needs.
- **Debt consolidation:** Replace multiple high-interest debts, such as credit cards and personal loans, with one low-cost EMI.
- **Education funding:** Finance higher studies without eroding retirement savings.
- **Medical or family needs:** Access large funds quickly against an asset you already own.

The common thread is that the borrowing serves a productive or unavoidable purpose and the low rate genuinely reduces your overall cost.

## Risks to Weigh Carefully

The low rate of LAP comes with real risk: your property is on the line. Consider these before signing:

- **Default means losing your property.** Only borrow what you can comfortably repay across the full tenure, accounting for income shocks.
- **Long tenures accrue large total interest.** Even at a low rate, 15 years of interest adds up. Prepay when you can.
- **Property valuation may disappoint.** Lenders often value conservatively, so the sanctioned amount may be lower than you expect.
- **Processing takes time.** Legal and technical verification of property makes LAP slower than unsecured loans, so plan ahead if you have a deadline.

## Balance Transfer and Top-Up

If your existing LAP carries a rate higher than the current market, transferring the outstanding to a cheaper lender can reduce your EMI. Weigh the transfer's processing and legal costs against the savings. Many lenders also offer a top-up over the transferred amount, giving you additional low-cost funds. Learn the mechanics in our [how to do a balance transfer](/knowledge-center/tutorials/how-to-do-a-balance-transfer) tutorial.

## LAP vs Personal Loan vs Business Loan

Choose LAP when you need a large amount, want the lowest possible rate and can pledge property. Choose a [personal loan](/loans/personal-loan) when you need a smaller sum quickly without collateral and can accept a higher rate. Choose a [business loan](/loans/business-loan) when the funding is specifically for enterprise needs and you prefer to keep personal assets unpledged. The right choice always balances cost, speed and the collateral you are willing to risk.
